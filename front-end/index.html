    Preventing tick bites

    • Stay on footpaths and avoid long grass.
    • In tick-prone areas, wear long sleeves and trousers tucked into your socks.
    • Choose light-coloured clothing, because ticks are easier to spot on it.
    • Use insect repellent on exposed skin.
    • At the end of the day, check yourself, your children (including the scalp and neck) and your pets for ticks.
    • Check skin folds such as armpits, groin and waistband.

    If you find a tick

    1. Grip the tick gently, as close to the skin as possible, using fine-tipped tweezers or a tick removal tool.
    2. Pull steadily away from the skin. Do not twist or squash it.
    3. Do not use heat, alcohol, petroleum jelly or other substances to make it let go.
    4. Wash the area with soap and water, and apply antiseptic cream around the bite.
    5. Watch the area over the following weeks for a rash or other symptoms.

    See your GP if you develop a circular, bull's-eye style rash (it can appear up to three months after a bite) or flu-like symptoms such as tiredness, aches, headache, fever, chills or a stiff neck. Tell them if you have been in woodland or heath, even if you don't remember a bite.
